Key findings
What the data tells us about attrition
⏰
Overtime is the #1 driver CRITICAL
Employees working overtime leave at 22.6% vs 3.2% for those who don't — a 7× difference. This is the single strongest predictor in the model.
💵
Low income amplifies risk HIGH
Employees earning under $3k/month have an 18.9% attrition rate vs 1.6% for those earning over $8k. Income is the strongest continuous predictor.
✈️
Frequent travel increases risk MODERATE
Frequent travelers show 10.9% attrition vs 6.9% for non-travelers. Business travel adds meaningful stress that compounds other risk factors.
📅
Tenure is protective POSITIVE
Each additional year at the company slightly reduces attrition risk. Employees who survive the first 3 years are significantly more likely to stay long-term.
😊
Satisfaction scores matter MODERATE
Job satisfaction, work-life balance, and environment satisfaction all independently reduce attrition risk. Low scores in any one area add compounding risk.
HR recommendations
Actionable interventions ranked by impact
1
Audit overtime policies immediately
Identify which teams consistently require overtime. Set caps, redistribute workload, or hire to address chronic understaffing. Target: reduce overtime workforce by 30%.
2
Compensation benchmarking
Review salaries for roles with highest attrition (Sales Exec, Research Scientist). Bring bottom quartile earners to market rate. Cost of replacement far exceeds a raise.
3
Run quarterly pulse surveys
Track satisfaction scores across all four dimensions (job, environment, relationship, work-life balance). Early warning system before employees decide to leave.
4
Deploy the risk predictor for new hires
Score new employees at 90-day, 6-month, and 1-year marks using this model. Focus retention conversations on employees scoring above 20% risk.
5
Travel policy flexibility
For frequent travelers, offer compensatory benefits: remote work days, flexible hours post-travel, or travel premium pay. Monitor if attrition rate drops below 8%.
